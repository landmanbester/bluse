# Track E — a morphology-only RFI score

**Measured 2026-09-03** on `aug_2026_workshop/features/all_features.parquet`:
2,014,055 hits, seven files, 444 observations. Reproduce everything with

```bash
bluse-score                 # ~4 min: fit, score, three catalogues, report, five figures
```

Spec: [`superpowers/specs/2026-09-03-track-e-design.md`](superpowers/specs/2026-09-03-track-e-design.md).

## 1. The question

The multi-beam spatial filter is this survey's strongest discriminant: a signal
in ≥32 of 64 coherent beams is local interference, a signal in one or two might
be on the sky. It is also **blind exactly where it matters.** It needs many
beams' worth of evidence, so it has nothing to say about a hit seen in one
beam — and a technosignature is a one-beam hit. So is a weak terrestrial
emitter that only clears the detection threshold at boresight.

The filter's verdicts are free labels. If a classifier can reproduce them from
the **stamp alone**, we get the filter's judgement on hits the filter cannot
judge.

Labels: `weak_label == 1` for ≥32 beams (1,311,629 hits), `0` for ≤2 beams
(291,735), `-1` for the 3–31 beam range the filter abstains on (410,691).
Group 5-fold on `obsid` throughout — hits in one observation share a pointing,
an RFI environment and a calibration, so a random row split leaks.

## 3. What could have made this worthless, and what each check found

A 0.99 AUC for predicting beam multiplicity has several boring explanations.
Each is closed by a block of `track_e_score.validate()` and pinned by a test in
`tests/workspace/test_track_e_numbers.py`.

| check | the boring explanation it kills | measured |
|---|---|---:|
| drop every zero-drift row | `x01_drift_residual` is NaN exactly at zero drift and P(RFI\|NaN) = 0.996 — a 29.6% freebie | **0.9903**, higher |
| AUC within each SNR decile, n-weighted | a signal must be bright to be *detected* in 32 beams, so the label is partly an SNR selection effect | **0.8956** |
| collapse to one row per signal | one emitter yields up to 64 near-duplicate rows, one per beam | **0.9890** |
| singleton signals only (234,809) | removes row duplication entirely | **0.9822** |
| hold out a whole band | memorised band-specific emitters rather than general morphology | L **0.9895**, UHF 0.8975, S 0.8962 |
| per file | one file carrying the result | 0.871–0.995, all six |

Brightness contributes and does not carry it. Duplication is not inflating it.
The zero-drift shortcut is not needed. It transfers to a band it has never seen.

### Which morphology says RFI

Permutation importance over the 12 stamp columns, one fold, 20k sample:

| column | drop in AUC | | column | drop in AUC |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| `x01_drift_residual` | **0.133** | | `f08_turning_bw_hz` | 0.004 |
| `f10_timeseries_std` | 0.052 | | `f13_redness` | 0.002 |
| `f11_spectrum_std` | 0.017 | | `f05_spectral_kurtosis` | 0.002 |
| `f06_bimodality` | 0.007 | | the remaining four | ≤0.001 |
| `f04_spectral_skew` | 0.007 | | | |

`x01_drift_residual` — how well the signal actually tracks its fitted drift
line — dominates. That **independently confirms** the mutual-information
ranking already recorded in `TODO.md` P1-4, which found `x01` first among 15
features with 4.9× the score of `f02_abs_drift`.

Note the difference between marginal and standalone importance: `f04` alone
reaches **0.9636 AUC** and `f06` alone 0.9531, but each adds ~0.007 given the
other eleven. The features are highly redundant, which is a finding in itself
and connects to the P2 redundancy panel.

## 4. Why the default is 12 features and not 16

`--features stamp` costs **0.0004 AUC** against `--features all` and buys three
things:

1. it cannot relearn the RFI frequency mask, so it is not circular with Track
   A's own band cut;
2. it is the variant that transfers to an unseen band — held-out L-band 0.9895
   for stamp against 0.9834 for all-16, and the metadata columns are what
   *stops* transfer, since a frequency learned in UHF means nothing in S;
3. "twelve numbers computed from the pixels" is a claim that can be defended.

Both ship and both are reported. Held-out UHF and S reverse the ordering
(0.8975 vs 0.9114; 0.8962 vs 0.9223), which is consistent with metadata helping
in-distribution and hurting out of it.

## 6. What the shortlist actually contains — read this before quoting 524

The shortlist is **not** 524 candidates. Those 524 hits collapse to about **49
distinct (file, 0.1 MHz) groups, the top ten holding 73% of them**:

| file | MHz | hits | observations |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `uhf_long` | 678.0 | 155 | 6 |
| `lband_long` | 960.0 | 73 | 1 |
| `lband_short` | 867.8–867.9 | 51 | 3 |
| `uhf_long` | 550.3 | 22 | 3 |
| `uhf_short` | 576.0 | 21 | 2 |

A handful of persistent, frequency-localised emitters seen many times over.
Plotting their stamps (`plots/*_stamps_candidates.png`) shows two distinct
morphological populations, **neither of them astrophysical**:

- **`lband_short` around 867.8 MHz** — bright first and last time rows across
  every channel, the same in all 24 lowest-scoring hits. That reads as an
  instrumental or edge effect, not a signal.
- **`uhf_long` at 599–678 MHz** — blocky, intermittent, multi-patch structures
  quite unlike a drifting carrier. Complex interference, and the reason it
  scores low is precisely that the model was trained on clean drifting lines.

**This is the score working, not failing.** An outlier ranking is supposed to
surface the things that do not fit, and it surfaced two populations nothing
else in the pipeline had isolated. It is also the clearest possible
demonstration of why a low score is not a detection: the top of the shortlist
is an instrument artefact.

## 7. `mk_sample_hits` — the model was right and the labels were wrong

That file carries **~25 hits per observation against ~2,931 elsewhere**. Beam
multiplicity is counted *within* a file, so at ~1% of the hit density it cannot
be counted properly: its `n_beams` tops out at **9** of up to 64 formed beams,
and it contains **zero** `weak_label == 1` rows. It is excluded from training
and still scored. A model trained on the other six files scores **94.2% of its
nominally-confined hits as RFI**.

That looked like a distribution-shift caveat. It is not — it is measurable, and
it was measured:

**8,116 hits appear in both `mk_sample_hits` and `lband_long`** — the same
`id`, byte-identical frequency, the same beam. The only thing that differs is
how many hits surround them.

| the same 8,116 hits | mean `n_beams` | median | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| counted in `mk_sample_hits` | **1.87** | 1 | 8 |
| counted in `lband_long` | **29.71** | 22 | 64 |

A **15.9× undercount.** Of the 6,141 of them that `mk_sample_hits` labels
confined (≤2 beams), `lband_long` puts **1,813 (29.5%) in ≥32 beams** — they
are multi-beam RFI — and confirms only 35.9% as genuinely confined.

So the model was not failing on this file. It was disagreeing with labels that
are wrong by construction, and it was disagreeing in the right direction.

**Two consequences for anyone using this data.** `mk_sample_hits` must not be
used for any beam-multiplicity-derived quantity — its `n_beams`, `beam_frac`,
`flag_multibeam` and `weak_label` are all artefacts of how sparsely it was
sampled. And Track A's **894 survivors** in that file are almost entirely
spurious: of the 848 that are duplicated into `lband_long`, only **15** survive
when the hits around them are present.

The general caveat still stands, now with a mechanism rather than a suspicion:
**a hit list selected differently from the one this score was fitted on will
not behave the same way** — and neither will the spatial filter it learned from.

*The first version of `validate()` had a defect here: it evaluated on
`mk_sample_hits` while excluding it from training, reporting 0.9795 where the
trainable population gives 0.9887 — quietly mixing distribution shift into the
headline. `evaluation_mask()` now names the rows a reported AUC may be computed
over.*

*A first attempt to test this by matching frequencies inside
`all_features.parquet` found nothing (0.6% within 2 Hz) and was reported as
unresolvable. That was wrong: the combined table deduplicates on `id`, so the
overlap is structurally invisible there. The per-file Track A catalogues keep
it, and there the answer is unambiguous.*

## 8. What would falsify this

Stated so the claim is refutable rather than merely supported:

1. **Ground truth.** No hit in this survey is confirmed clean. Every number
   here measures agreement with the spatial filter, which is a good instrument
   and not a truth. Synthetic injections (P2) would give a real objective
   function and are the single most valuable follow-up.
2. **The monotonicity could be an SNR gradient.** Faint hits are detected in
   fewer beams *and* have less structure. The within-decile AUC of 0.8956
   argues against it but does not eliminate it; the decisive test is scoring
   injected signals at matched SNR.
3. **A held-out band drops to 0.90.** If a genuinely new band scored at chance,
   the "general morphology" claim would fail. It does not, but 0.90 against
   0.99 in-band is the honest size of the transfer penalty.
4. **The shortlist may be entirely instrumental.** §6 found two artefact
   populations at the top of it. If every group in it resolves the same way,
   the score's value is real but confined to pruning, with no discovery value.

## 9. Not done

- **Calibration.** The score is used as a ranking, and under
  positive-unlabelled framing a calibrated P(RFI) is not identifiable. Ranking
  metrics only.
- **SHAP.** Permutation importance answers the question with no new dependency.
- **Per-band models.** Cross-band transfer is measured; retraining per band
  would improve in-band numbers and remove the transfer evidence.
- **Feeding the score back into Track A as a cut.** It is a ranking, and
  turning it into a cut needs an operating point that needs §8's ground truth.
