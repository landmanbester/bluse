# Track E — a morphology-only RFI score

**Measured 2026-09-03** on `aug_2026_workshop/features/all_features.parquet`
(renamed later that day to `all_globular_features.parquet` — same contents; see
`aug_2026_workshop/features/format.md`):
2,014,055 hits, seven files, 444 observations. **float64 throughout, and
the score averaged over three seeds** — §9 says why neither is optional.
Reproduce everything with

```bash
bluse-score                 # ~9 min: fit, score, three catalogues, report, five figures
```

Spec: [`superpowers/specs/2026-09-03-track-e-design.md`](superpowers/specs/2026-09-03-track-e-design.md).
Definitions: [`../NOMENCLATURE.md`](../NOMENCLATURE.md#rfi-score).

## 1. The question

The multi-beam spatial filter is this survey's strongest discriminant: a signal
in ≥32 of 64 coherent beams is local interference, a signal in one or two might
be on the sky. It is also **blind exactly where it matters.** It needs many
beams' worth of evidence, so it has nothing to say about a hit seen in one
beam — and a technosignature is a one-beam hit. So is a weak terrestrial
emitter that only clears the detection threshold at boresight.

The filter's verdicts are free labels. If a classifier can reproduce them from
the **stamp alone**, we get the filter's judgement on hits the filter cannot
judge.

Labels: `weak_label == 1` for ≥32 beams (1,311,629 hits), `0` for ≤2 beams
(291,735), `-1` for the 3–31 beam range the filter abstains on (410,691).
Group 5-fold on `obsid` throughout — hits in one observation share a pointing,
an RFI environment and a calibration, so a random row split leaks.

## 2. The result

| feature set | n | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| metadata + stamp | 16 | **0.9911** | 0.9973 |
| **stamp morphology** — the shipped default | 12 | **0.9899** | 0.9969 |
| metadata only | 4 | 0.9845 | 0.9954 |
| **Track A's entire flag set** | 6 | **0.9373** | 0.9839 |
| `f01_frequency` alone | 1 | 0.8757 | — |
| raw `snr` alone | 1 | 0.7398 | — |

### Read that table correctly — the obvious reading is wrong

The tempting headline is "twelve numbers from the stamp pixels beat the six
hand-built flags of the classical filter by 0.053." It is arithmetically true
and **it misattributes almost all of the gap.** Decomposed:

| step | ΔAUC | share of the gap |
|---|---:|---:|
| `flags` → `meta`: stop binarising | **+0.0471** | **90%** |
| `meta` → `stamp`: use the pixels instead | +0.0054 | 10% |

Track A's six flags are *thresholds* on quantities the `meta` set holds
continuously — `flag_rfi_band` on frequency, `flag_zero_drift` and
`flag_drift_high` on drift, `flag_snr_low`/`flag_snr_high` on SNR. Handing the
model the continuous versions recovers 0.047, and handing it the flags **as
well** buys a further **+0.0012**: the continuous quantities essentially
subsume their own thresholds.

So the honest pair of claims is:

1. **The stamp alone carries what the beams carry.** Twelve numbers computed
   from the pixels — no frequency, no drift rate, no SNR — reach **0.9899**,
   above all four metadata columns together (0.9845) and within 0.0012 of
   everything combined (0.9911). That is the claim Track E rests on, and it is
   what makes the score available on a hit where the beams cannot help.
2. **Thresholding is expensive, and that is a finding about the classical
   filter.** Replacing Track A's six booleans with the four continuous
   quantities they threshold is worth **0.047 AUC** — nine times what
   morphology adds on top. See §10.

The flag row is worth a second look: it is **0.937311 in all eight
configurations tested** — both dtypes × {one seed at each of 0, 1, 2} ∪ {three
seeds} — identical to six decimal places, not merely close. Six boolean columns
have two distinct values each, so no bin edge can move under them. It is an
unplanned control, and it says the variation in every other row of this table is
bin-edge placement rather than anything about the data.

### The figure that carries it

The model is trained **only** on ≤2 beams and ≥32 beams. Scored on the
untrained middle:

| `n_beams` | 1 | 2 | **3** | **4–5** | **6–9** | **10–17** | **18–32** | 33–49 | 50–65 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| mean score | 0.060 | 0.120 | **0.181** | **0.270** | **0.443** | **0.710** | **0.900** | 0.959 | 0.985 |
| trained? | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |

422,480 hits in five bins the model never saw, ordered correctly and smoothly.
A fitted decision boundary has no reason to interpolate. `plots/track_e_monotonicity.png`.

## 3. What could have made this worthless, and what each check found

A 0.99 AUC for predicting beam multiplicity has several boring explanations.
Each is closed by a block of `track_e_score.validate()` and pinned by a test in
`tests/workspace/test_track_e_numbers.py`.

| check | the boring explanation it kills | measured |
|---|---|---:|
| drop every zero-drift row | `x01_drift_residual` is NaN exactly at zero drift and P(RFI\|NaN) = 0.996 — a 29.6% freebie | **0.9927**, higher |
| AUC within each SNR decile, n-weighted | a signal must be bright to be *detected* in 32 beams, so the label is partly an SNR selection effect | **0.8934** |
| collapse to one row per signal | one emitter yields up to 64 near-duplicate rows, one per beam | **0.9894** |
| singleton signals only (234,809) | removes row duplication entirely | **0.9835** |
| hold out a whole band | memorised band-specific emitters rather than general morphology | L **0.9911**, UHF 0.8976, S 0.8962 |
| per file | one file carrying the result | 0.871–0.995, all six |

Brightness contributes and does not carry it. Duplication is not inflating it.
The zero-drift shortcut is not needed. It transfers to a band it has never seen.

### Which morphology says RFI

Permutation importance over the 12 stamp columns, one fold, 20k sample:

| column | drop in AUC | | column | drop in AUC |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| `x01_drift_residual` | **0.121** | | `f04_spectral_skew` | 0.013 |
| `f10_timeseries_std` | 0.059 | | `f08_turning_bw_hz` | 0.008 |
| `f11_spectrum_std` | 0.047 | | `f13_redness` | 0.004 |
| `f06_bimodality` | 0.015 | | the remaining five | ≤0.003 |
| `f05_spectral_kurtosis` | 0.013 | | | |

`x01_drift_residual` — how well the signal actually tracks its fitted drift
line — dominates. That **independently confirms** the mutual-information
ranking already recorded in `TODO.md` P1-4, which found `x01` first among 15
features with 4.9× the score of `f02_abs_drift`.

Note the difference between marginal and standalone importance: `f04` alone
reaches **0.9667 AUC** and `f06` alone 0.9590 — but each adds only ~0.014 given
the other eleven, while `x01`, which is *weaker* alone (0.9380), is by far the
largest marginal contributor. The features are highly redundant and `x01`
carries something the others do not. Both are findings in themselves, and both
connect to the P2 redundancy panel.

## 4. Why the default is 12 features and not 16 — restated honestly

**The 16-feature model is now the better one on this survey: 0.9911 against
0.9899.** At float32 with a single seed the ordering was the other way round
(0.9887 against 0.9884) and the default cost 0.0004 AUC. It now costs
**0.0012**, three times as much, and the ordering has flipped. The
justification has to be restated rather than reused.

What survives, and it is the argument that always mattered:

**`--features stamp` cannot relearn the RFI frequency mask.** Track A's first
cut is a known-RFI frequency mask, so a model holding `f01_frequency` partly
re-derives it — and the headline claim of this document is that morphology
beats *that filter's own flags*. Including frequency would make the comparison
partly circular. It also matters for how the score is used: it is applied to
Track A **survivors**, which by construction sit outside the mask, so a
frequency-using model is extrapolating into exactly the regions where it saw
fewest positives.

**What no longer survives** is the transfer argument. Held-out band, stamp
against all-16: L **0.9911 / 0.9872**, UHF 0.8976 / **0.9127**, S 0.8962 /
**0.9157**. Stamp wins one band of three, not three of three as at single-seed
float32. Metadata helps out of band more than it hurts, and the earlier claim
that it "stops transfer" is not supported.

So: stamp stays the default and the headline, on non-circularity alone, and
0.9911 is reported as the better in-band number. `--features all` ships and is
the right choice for anyone who does not need the non-circular claim.
## 5. The deliverable

`scores/candidates.csv` — every Track A survivor, ranked, with a verdict:

| verdict | n | | what it means |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **pruned** (>0.9) | **2,988** | 65.5% | passed the classical filter, looks exactly like multi-beam RFI |
| uncertain | 1,053 | 23.1% | no verdict either way |
| shortlist (<0.1) | 524 | 11.5% | does not resemble this survey's multi-beam RFI |

**Two thirds of the vetting list ranked to the bottom, with a per-hit reason.**
That is the actionable output — an *ordering*, not yet a cut. Turning it into a
cut needs an operating point, and an operating point needs the ground truth §8
asks for.

**Do not scale this to the full survey by the id range.** The tempting
arithmetic — our 2,014,055 hits span ids 1–35,904,223, so we hold 5.6% and
4,565 survivors implies ~81,000 survey-wide — does not hold. Our hits are
distributed over that range with a **107× ratio between the densest and
sparsest decile** (56% of them fall in the first tenth of the range), so this
is not a uniform draw, and the survivor *rate* varies by an order of magnitude
between files anyway (0.09% in `lband_short`, 0.76% in `uhf_long`). The note in
`AGENTS.md` offers 5.6% as a caution about how representative our findings are,
not as a multiplier. The honest statement is that the full survey is much
larger than this set and we cannot say by how much from what we hold.

Also written: `contrarian.csv` (**1,515** hits in ≥32 beams that score clean —
the filter and morphology disagreeing in the one direction "the filter is
conservative" cannot explain; at a single seed this set was 3,303, and §9
explains why roughly half of it was never real) and `ambiguous.csv` (411,898 hits in 3–31 beams,
where the filter abstains and this is the only verdict available).

## 6. What the shortlist actually contains — read this before quoting 524

The shortlist is **not** 524 candidates. Those 524 hits collapse to **48
distinct (file, 0.1 MHz) groups, the top ten holding 73% of them**:

| file | MHz | hits | observations |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `uhf_long` | 678.0 | 157 | 6 |
| `lband_long` | 960.0 | 73 | 1 |
| `lband_short` | 867.8–867.9 | 51 | 3 |
| `uhf_long` | 550.3 | 22 | 3 |
| `uhf_short` | 576.0 | 22 | 3 |

A handful of persistent, frequency-localised emitters seen many times over.
Plotting their stamps (`plots/*_stamps_candidates.png`) shows two distinct
morphological populations, **neither of them astrophysical**:

- **`lband_short` around 867.8 MHz** — bright first and last time rows across
  every channel, the same in all 24 lowest-scoring hits. That reads as an
  instrumental or edge effect, not a signal.
- **`uhf_long` at 599–678 MHz** — blocky, intermittent, multi-patch structures
  quite unlike a drifting carrier. Complex interference, and the reason it
  scores low is precisely that the model was trained on clean drifting lines.

**This is the score working, not failing.** An outlier ranking is supposed to
surface the things that do not fit, and it surfaced two populations nothing
else in the pipeline had isolated. It is also the clearest possible
demonstration of why a low score is not a detection: the top of the shortlist
is an instrument artefact.

## 7. `mk_sample_hits` — the model was right and the labels were wrong

That file carries **~25 hits per observation against ~2,931 elsewhere**. Beam
multiplicity is counted *within* a file, so at ~1% of the hit density it cannot
be counted properly: its `n_beams` tops out at **9** of up to 64 formed beams,
and it contains **zero** `weak_label == 1` rows. It is excluded from training
and still scored. A model trained on the other six files scores **94.2% of its
nominally-confined hits as RFI**.

That looked like a distribution-shift caveat. It is not — it is measurable, and
it was measured:

**8,116 hits appear in both `mk_sample_hits` and `lband_long`** — the same
`id`, byte-identical frequency, the same beam. The only thing that differs is
how many hits surround them.

| the same 8,116 hits | mean `n_beams` | median | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| counted in `mk_sample_hits` | **1.87** | 1 | 8 |
| counted in `lband_long` | **29.71** | 22 | 64 |

A **15.9× undercount.** Of the 6,141 of them that `mk_sample_hits` labels
confined (≤2 beams), `lband_long` puts **1,813 (29.5%) in ≥32 beams** — they
are multi-beam RFI — and confirms only 35.9% as genuinely confined.

So the model was not failing on this file. It was disagreeing with labels that
are wrong by construction, and it was disagreeing in the right direction.

**Two consequences for anyone using this data.** `mk_sample_hits` must not be
used for any beam-multiplicity-derived quantity — its `n_beams`, `beam_frac`,
`flag_multibeam` and `weak_label` are all artefacts of how sparsely it was
sampled. And Track A's **894 survivors** in that file are almost entirely
spurious: of the 848 that are duplicated into `lband_long`, only **15** survive
when the hits around them are present.

The general caveat still stands, now with a mechanism rather than a suspicion:
**a hit list selected differently from the one this score was fitted on will
not behave the same way** — and neither will the spatial filter it learned from.

*The first version of `validate()` had a defect here: it evaluated on
`mk_sample_hits` while excluding it from training, reporting 0.9795 where the
trainable population gave 0.9887 (both at the single-seed float32 settings
of the time; the equivalent pair now is 0.9821 and 0.9899) — quietly mixing distribution shift into the
headline. `evaluation_mask()` now names the rows a reported AUC may be computed
over.*

*A first attempt to test this by matching frequencies inside
`all_features.parquet` found nothing (0.6% within 2 Hz) and was reported as
unresolvable. That was wrong: the combined table deduplicates on `id`, so the
overlap is structurally invisible there. The per-file Track A catalogues keep
it, and there the answer is unambiguous.*

## 8. What would falsify this

Stated so the claim is refutable rather than merely supported:

1. ~~**Ground truth.**~~ — **PARTLY ANSWERED**, see
   [`injections-2026-09.md`](injections-2026-09.md). No hit in this survey is
   confirmed clean, so every number here measures agreement with the spatial
   filter. Synthetic injections supply the missing direction, and what they
   returned qualifies §5 rather than confirming it:
   - the shipped `shortlist_below=0.1` **is** the best threshold swept, by
     injected-signal kept per survivor admitted — but it keeps only ~55% of
     injected signals in the favourable regime;
   - **"pruning is unaffected" below is wrong.** At catalogue SNR ≈51 with no
     drift, **91%** of injected real signals land above the 0.9 pruning
     threshold, against a 33% control. A bright non-drifting sky signal would
     be actively discarded.
2. ~~**The monotonicity could be an SNR gradient.**~~ — **ANSWERED, and not in
   this score's favour.** Injected signals at matched SNR show the paired shift
   *reversing* above catalogue SNR ≈10 (zero drift) to ≈30 (0.3 Hz/s): a
   brighter real signal is scored as *more* RFI-like. It is not an artefact of
   a noiseless injection — a Gamma-fluctuating version reproduces it to within a
   few points. The within-decile AUC of 0.8934 said brightness contributes and
   does not carry the result; the injections say it carries it at the bright
   end.
3. **A held-out band drops to 0.90.** If a genuinely new band scored at chance,
   the "general morphology" claim would fail. It does not, but 0.90 against
   0.99 in-band is the honest size of the transfer penalty.
4. **The shortlist may be entirely instrumental.** §6 found two artefact
   populations at the top of it. If every group in it resolves the same way,
   the score's value is real but confined to pruning, with no discovery value.

## 9. Numerical precision and seed stability

Added because the question was asked directly: *does float32 cost us anything?*
The answer turned out to be less interesting than what the experiment found
next.

### float32 against float64

Same process, same data, same folds, `stamp` model, seed 0:

| | float32 | float64 |
|---|---:|---:|
| ROC-AUC | 0.98872 | 0.98911 |
| per-hit scores identical | — | 2.2% |
| hits differing by >0.1 | — | 11,622 |
| `contrarian` count | 3,303 | 2,972 |

The headline is stable to 4×10⁻⁴. **The per-hit scores are not.** Only 2.2% are
bit-identical and 11,622 hits move by more than 0.1. That is not numerical
instability in the usual sense: `HistGradientBoosting` bins each column into
256 levels and upcasts to float64 internally, so rounding the input only
changes *which side of a bin edge* a value falls on — and 300 boosting rounds
amplify one flipped split into a visibly different model. Both models are
equally valid; they are simply different models.

**float64 is what ships**, on three grounds: every feature column in the
parquet is float64 and every other science path in this package asks for
float64 explicitly, so Track E was the only place narrowing; it costs nothing
measurable (27 s either way); and it is **3.5× more seed-stable**
(AUC σ = 0.000038 against 0.000133 over seeds 0–2).

### The finding underneath: the seed matters more than the dtype

Running both dtypes across seeds 0–2 shows precision is not special. It is
ordinary model churn, and *less* of it than changing the seed:

| | seed vs seed (float64) | float32 vs float64 (same seed) |
|---|---:|---:|
| shortlist membership, Jaccard | **0.931** | 0.955 |
| mean per-hit \|Δscore\| | 5.7×10⁻³ | 4.4×10⁻³ |

The mechanism is the same in both cases and it is worth naming: the bin edges
come from a **random 200,000-row subsample**, so the seed decides them just as
surely as rounding does.

Across seeds 0–2 at float64, single-seed:

| quantity | mean | range |
|---|---:|---|
| ROC-AUC | 0.98916 | ±0.00004 |
| `pruned` | 2,982 | 2,967 – 2,998 |
| `shortlist` | 529 | 528 – 532 |
| **`contrarian`** | **3,106** | **2,972 – 3,368** |

So the AUC was always safe to quote to four decimals, and the **counts were
not**. `contrarian` in particular swings ±13% on the seed alone.

### What shipped as a result

`fit_score` now **averages over three seeds by default** (`--seeds N`,
`--seeds 1` for the old behaviour). It costs 3 × 27 s and it:

- raises ROC-AUC from 0.9892 to **0.9899**;
- stabilises every count;
- **halves the `contrarian` set, 3,303 → 1,515.**

**That last one is asserted, not established, and it is the weak point of this
section.** The reading above — that averaging removed hits which only fell
below 0.1 because of one seed's bin edges — is consistent with everything
measured, and it is not the only reading. A hit that scores 0.05 under one seed
and 0.3 under another is *either* churn *or* a genuinely marginal case that
averaging has smoothed away, and **nothing in this data distinguishes the two.**
Settling it needs an operating point, which needs the ground truth §8 asks for.

Until then the 1,515 should be read as the hits that are contrarian *robustly*,
and the 1,788 that averaging dropped as unexamined rather than disproved. The
single-seed set is one command away (`bluse-score --seeds 1`) if anyone wants
to look at the difference.

Averaging does not weaken the no-leak property: every seed uses the same
`GroupKFold` split, so a row's score is the mean of models none of which saw
its observation.

**The control that confirms all of this** is the `flags` row of §2. Six boolean
columns have two distinct values each, so no bin edge can move under them, and
that row reads **0.937311 in all eight configurations tested** — both dtypes ×
seeds {0}, {1}, {2} and {0,1,2} — identical to six decimal places rather than
merely close. Every other number in this document moved; that one could not,
and did not.

## 10. A finding about Track A, not about Track E

The decomposition in §2 is worth pulling out, because it is the most directly
actionable thing here and it is about the classical filter rather than the
model.

| feature set | n | ROC-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|
| Track A's six flags | 6 | 0.9373 |
| the four continuous quantities they threshold | 4 | **0.9845** |
| both together | 10 | 0.9856 |

**Binarising costs 0.047 AUC**, and giving the model the flags on top of the
continuous quantities recovers only 0.0012 of it — so the thresholds carry
essentially nothing their own inputs do not.

This is not an argument for deleting the cuts. Track A's flags exist to make a
*decision* with a defensible, documented provenance (SARAO tables, ITU
allocations, published SNR windows), and a threshold is what a decision needs.
It is an argument that **anything downstream which consumes the flags should
consume the continuous quantities instead** — a ranking, a classifier, a
diagnostic. Where the pipeline turns 1,700 MHz into one bit, it is discarding
nine tenths of what separates interference from everything else.

Cheap to check and cheap to act on, which is why it is here rather than in the
backlog.

## 11. Not done

- **Calibration.** The score is used as a ranking, and under
  positive-unlabelled framing a calibrated P(RFI) is not identifiable. Ranking
  metrics only.
- **SHAP.** Permutation importance answers the question with no new dependency.
- **Per-band models.** Cross-band transfer is measured; retraining per band
  would improve in-band numbers and remove the transfer evidence.
- **Feeding the score back into Track A as a cut.** It is a ranking, and
  turning it into a cut needs an operating point that needs §8's ground truth.
